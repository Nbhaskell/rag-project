# Tidepool Trails: Visitor Guide

Tidepool Trails is a fictional coastal nature preserve covering 640 acres along the Pacific shoreline. It was established in 1987 and is managed by the Tidepool Conservancy, a nonprofit with 14 full-time rangers.

## Hours and Admission

The preserve is open daily from 7:00 AM to sunset. The visitor center is open from 9:00 AM to 4:00 PM, Wednesday through Sunday, and is closed Monday and Tuesday.

Admission is $8 per adult, $4 for children ages 6 to 12, and free for children under 6. Annual passes cost $60 for an individual and $95 for a household. Conservancy members enter free. Parking is free in Lot A but costs $5 per day in Lot B, which is closer to the beach.

## The Four Main Trails

1. **Kelp Loop** is 1.2 miles, flat, and wheelchair accessible. It takes about 40 minutes and passes the main tidepool viewing area.
2. **Heron Ridge Trail** is 3.5 miles with 410 feet of elevation gain. It takes about 2 hours and is the best trail for spotting great blue herons, which nest there from March to June.
3. **Driftwood Run** is 2.4 miles along the beach. It is only passable at low tide, so check the tide chart posted at the visitor center before you go.
4. **Fogbank Summit** is the hardest trail at 6.1 miles and 1,150 feet of elevation gain. It takes 4 to 5 hours round trip. Hikers must sign in at the trailhead register and carry at least 2 liters of water.

## Tidepool Rules

Tidepools are fragile habitats, so please follow these rules:
- Look and touch gently with one wet finger only. Never pick up sea stars, anemones, or crabs.
- Do not remove shells, rocks, or any living thing from the preserve.
- Stay on the marked rock paths and avoid stepping on barnacles or mussel beds.
- Visit within two hours of low tide for the best viewing. The lowest tides of the year happen in early summer.

## Wildlife You Might See

Harbor seals rest on the offshore rocks and are most visible at mid-morning. Gray whales pass the headland during their migrations, southbound in December and January and northbound in March and April. The tidepools hold purple ochre sea stars, green anemones, hermit crabs, and small sculpin fish. Black oystercatchers, with their bright orange beaks, are common along the rocky shoreline.

## Rules and Safety

- Dogs are allowed only on the Kelp Loop and must be on a leash no longer than 6 feet. Dogs are not permitted on the other trails or beaches because of nesting shorebirds.
- No drones, no fires, and no camping. Smoking is not permitted anywhere in the preserve.
- Stay at least 50 yards from resting seals. If a seal pup appears abandoned, do not touch it. Call the ranger hotline at 555-0142.
- Watch the waves. Sneaker waves can reach well above the high-tide line, and visitors should never turn their back on the ocean.

## Volunteering

The Conservancy runs volunteer beach cleanups on the first Saturday of every month at 9:00 AM. Volunteers should bring gloves and a reusable water bottle. Trail stewards commit to one four-hour shift per month and receive a free annual pass.
